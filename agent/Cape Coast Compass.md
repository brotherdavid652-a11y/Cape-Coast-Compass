# Cape Coast Compass: Complete Project Documentation

> Consolidated project brief, implemented website, operating proposal and launch roadmap. This document covers the entire project, not only the essay. Features described as proposed or pending are not currently operating services.

## 1. Project overview

Cape Coast Compass is the working name for a tourism business and website focused on Cape Coast, Ghana. The business would connect visitors with attractions and handle the journey: collect customers within Cape Coast, transport them to their chosen site or sites, handle agreed admission payments where applicable, and return them to the original pickup point.

The founder intends to work directly with attraction managers and authorities, serving as the communication link between them and customers. The website is also a demonstration to show managers during initial partnership discussions. No partnerships, admission agreements or commercial terms are represented as confirmed.

Website: https://cape-coast-compass.brotherdavid652.chatgpt.site/

## 2. Purpose and initial scope

The project makes it easier to discover attractions and assemble a proposed visit. It combines tourism information with a trip planner, rather than offering only a directory of places.

The initial pickup service is limited to Cape Coast. Customers may choose one or more supported city attractions for the same proposed trip. Nearby destinations appear separately for future planning and require separate arrangements; they are not included in the initial city booking selection.

Intended visitors include individuals, families and groups already in Cape Coast who want transport and attraction entry coordinated. These are intended audiences, not validated customer segments or evidence of demand.

## 3. Proposed service and responsibilities

The proposed service includes:

- Communicating with visitors and attraction managers.
- Agreeing on the date, group size, pickup point and itinerary.
- Collecting visitors within Cape Coast.
- Transporting them to agreed attractions.
- Handling applicable admission payments under agreed venue arrangements.
- Returning visitors to their original pickup point at the agreed time.

Meals, optional activities and guiding services are not automatically included. Some listed places do not charge admission. Availability, transport costs, entry charges and return time must be confirmed before a customer commits.

Attraction managers retain control of access, visiting hours, site rules, capacity and any guiding services. The operator is responsible for explaining the agreed arrangements and coordinating the journey. Transport provision, staffing and settlement procedures remain to be established.

## 4. Business and partnership model

The founder plans to visit attraction managers in person and present the website as evidence of the proposed service. Discussions should establish access permission, admission categories, group arrangements, payment and receipt procedures, cancellation terms, visitor capacity and approved photographs.

No final pricing model has been set. A future price could include transport, admission where applicable and a disclosed service charge, or another arrangement agreed with venues. No commission percentage, revenue forecast or profit projection has been established.

The website must not imply endorsement or affiliation before it is agreed. Any future partnership claims, prices or service guarantees should reflect actual operating arrangements.

## 7. Photography and attraction information

The project sourced 56 images across 24 listings. Eight listings still await verified photographs; some photographed listings contain only one image. Galleries provide captions and credit information, with previous/next controls and thumbnails where appropriate.

Photos must depict the correct place. Historical official-visit images are contextualised, and gallery artwork images are distinguished from photographs of exhibition rooms. Missing images are represented by clear placeholders rather than unrelated pictures.

The visible “Information source ↗” links were removed from all 32 attraction pages at the user's request. Photograph credits remain. Removing a visible information-source link does not establish that access or venue details have been independently confirmed. Image permissions, particularly for reference photographs without open reuse licences, remain a partnership and launch consideration.

## 8. Visitor journey and trip planning

A visitor can browse the homepage or directory, open an attraction page, read its information and view its gallery. The visit form appears below the attraction information, with a prominent planning button leading to it.

For a supported city attraction, opening its page adds it to the proposed trip if it is not already selected. The visitor can select additional city attractions or remove selections in the form. Nearby destinations require separate arrangements and are not automatically added to the city planner.

The form collects:

- One or more supported Cape Coast attractions.
- A preferred date.
- Number of visitors.
- Pickup point within Cape Coast.

The form requires at least one attraction and a pickup point, and provides date and group-size validation. The existing demonstration permits a group-size input from 1 to 100; this is a form limit, not confirmed vehicle or venue capacity.

Selections and entered details are retained across pages in the current browser session. “Review my visit” opens the review page with all selected stops, date, visitor count and pickup details. Visitors can return to edit their trip.

Multiple selected attractions do not constitute an optimised route or confirmed schedule. The operator must check opening hours, travel time, time at each venue, transport capacity and return arrangements.

## 9. Permission-based pickup location

A **Use my current location** button appears beside the pickup field on the homepage and attraction-page forms. It requests a location reading only after the visitor clicks it. The browser controls the permission decision.

When permission is available and detection succeeds, the website fills the field with latitude and longitude and displays the estimated accuracy. The visitor may edit the field to provide an intended collection point or a nearby landmark. The feature does not convert coordinates into a street address.

The implementation requests a fresh, high-accuracy reading with a 20-second timeout. Permission denial, unavailable location and timeout conditions show explanatory messages, preserve an existing pickup entry and allow manual entry.

The feature does not continuously track visitors. It does not automatically verify that coordinates fall within Cape Coast, that the visitor is at a safe pickup point, or that a vehicle can reach that point. These require confirmation.

At present the location fills the local trip form and is retained with the trip in the browser session. No booking service receives it because booking submission has not been connected.

## 10. Google Maps and assistant decisions

An earlier route helper could search for attractions, add stops and open driving directions in Google Maps. It also offered separate route legs for longer itineraries. The user subsequently requested the removal of Google Maps navigation.

The external Google Maps navigation, route helper and pickup-map links were therefore removed. The current location button works without sending visitors to Google Maps. There is no embedded live map, automatic route optimisation, live travel-time calculation or AI chatbot in the current website. The requested location assistance is implemented as a permission-based browser feature rather than a conversational bot.

## 11. Trip review, booking and card payment

Card payment is the intended payment method. The user explicitly deferred connecting a provider and requested the underlying website flow first.

The review page shows selected attractions, preferred date, group size and pickup point. Transport is shown as awaiting a quote, admission as requiring confirmation and the total as unconfirmed. The card-payment button is unavailable.

The website currently does **not**:

- Submit a booking request to the operator.
- Reserve admission or transport.
- Check live availability.
- Calculate or charge a final price.
- Collect card details or process payments.
- Send booking confirmations, receipts or operator notifications.

A production booking flow must connect the reviewed request to an operator process, agreed availability, a final price and a payment provider. Confirmation and payment status should be linked to the same booking before the service is presented as available to buy.

## 12. Design and usability

The website uses a navy, cream and gold visual style with prominent photography and readable attraction descriptions. Refinements include rounded attraction cards, photo-count badges, consistent placeholders, clearer spacing, stronger planning buttons and mobile-friendly forms.

Gallery controls, form labels, focus styles and status messages support navigation and feedback. Mobile navigation remains visible. The location button is distinct from the review action so visitors understand that sharing a location does not book a trip.

The latest refinements removed information-source links and improved the attraction-page planning area. The preview notice and pending-payment language remain necessary because the operational connections are not complete.

## 13. Technical implementation

The implemented site is a static website built from HTML, CSS and JavaScript. It is published through the existing Sites project. There is no connected application database, booking API or payment backend in the current implementation.

| File or component | Role |
| --- | --- |
| index.html | Homepage and shared planner. |
| attractions.html | City directory and nearby listings. |
| places/*.html | 32 individual attraction pages. |
| payment.html | Trip review and inactive card-payment presentation. |
| partners.html | Partnership proposal and FAQs. |
| plan.html | Redirect to the homepage planner. |
| style.css | Shared presentation and responsive styling. |
| attractions.json | Directory descriptions and research/access notes. |
| galleries.js | Attraction metadata, page slugs and photo data. |
| trip.js | Selection, form validation, session persistence, filtering, gallery interactions and review rendering. |
| pickup-location.js | Click-triggered geolocation and manual-entry fallback. |
| map-assistant.js | Retired; external Google Maps navigation removed. |
| .openai/hosting.json | Static hosting configuration pointing to dist. |

Trip state uses browser sessionStorage under the key cape-compass-trip-v1. It stores the selected attraction identifiers, date, group size and pickup text. This is session-level browser persistence, not a customer account or durable booking record. Location access uses the browser Geolocation API on HTTPS and remains subject to browser/device permissions.

The older app.js and gallery.js files remain in the static directory from earlier iterations; current multi-page documents use trip.js. Any future cleanup should verify active references before removing legacy files.

## 14. Validation completed and limits

Implementation checks have covered attraction preselection, multiple-stop review, cross-page session persistence, empty-trip validation and internal links across the 32 attraction pages. Earlier gallery testing checked photo navigation and the transition into visit planning.

Location tests use simulated browser responses to verify no automatic request on load, click-triggered access, coordinate insertion, permission-denied handling and timeout handling. These checks do not substitute for field testing actual GPS accuracy or permission behaviour on visitors' devices.

Earlier Google Maps route tests are historical and no longer describe an active feature. Deployment success confirms publication, not that every attraction is available or that the business is ready to accept bookings.

## 16. Work required before launch

First, inspect the listed venues and confirm which can be included in the initial service. Agree access, admission, capacity, opening times, guiding arrangements and image permissions with the relevant managers.

Second, establish transport arrangements, pickup coverage, contact procedures, return times and a realistic approach to multiple-stop itineraries. Confirm the customer price and explain which items are included or optional.

Third, implement booking submission and an operator workflow for reviewing requests, confirming availability and communicating a quote. Decide how customers will receive confirmations, changes and cancellation information. No contact channel or booking notification system should be assumed to exist until connected.

Fourth, integrate the selected card-payment provider, connect payment results to bookings and implement appropriate handling for unsuccessful payments, cancellations and refunds. The current website must continue to state that payments are unavailable until this work is complete.

Finally, complete customer-facing terms and information about the use and retention of pickup details, test the full flow on real devices, resolve remaining photo/access gaps and confirm that the site's audience settings match the intended launch. These are future implementation needs, not completed features.

## 17. Decisions preserved from the project

- Cape Coast is the starting market; initial pickups are within Cape Coast.
- The service combines transport, applicable admission handling and return to the original pickup point.
- Attraction managers are approached directly before partnerships are claimed.
- The final structure is multi-page, superseding the earlier single-page request.
- Visitors can select more than one supported city attraction in a trip.
- Visit forms appear with attraction information; the shared planner also remains on the homepage.
- Card payment is planned, with the provider connection deferred.
- Visitors can voluntarily fill the pickup field from their current location.
- External Google Maps navigation has been removed.
- Visible Information source links have been removed from attraction pages.
- Missing photos, uncertain access and unavailable booking/payment connections must be represented honestly.
